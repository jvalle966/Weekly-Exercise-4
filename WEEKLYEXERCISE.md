## What is civic technology, and how can Smart Cities benefit from it?
In general terms, civic technology can be understood as the use of digital technologies to address public problems, improve interactions between residents and government, and increase opportunities for civic participation. In theory, civic technology is centered around the relationship between government and the public. Examples of this can include open-data platforms, 311 applications, participatory budgeting platforms, interactive maps, online public engagement tools, and applications created by community organizations or residents. 

In the context of smart cities, civic technology can be particularly valuable because it introduces a more citizen-centered approach to technological innovation. Smart cities commonly use technology like sensors, data, and digital infrastructure to improve public services and address problems such as transportation, sustainability, public safety, and infrastructure. However, simply introducing advanced thecnology does not necessarily ensure that a city is responding to the needs of its residents. Wilson and Chakraborty (2019) argue that civic technology can help democratize Smart Cities by using open data and collaborative, problem-centered approaches to address challenges that may otherwise be overlooked by government. 

In this case, civic technology can then help Smart Cities connect technological innovation with public participation. In practice, this might look like open data platforms allowing residents, researchers, and community organizations to analyze government information and identify disparities or service problems. Research suggests that digital participation can expand the number of geographic range of people who can participate in government processes and make it easier for governments to manage public input (OECD, 2025). That said, civic technology can be used to help ensure that Smart Cities aren't just technology-driven, but instead, incorporate technology in ways that improve the lives of residents. It has the potential to increase transparency, participation, responsiveness, and collaboration. Even with all of these benefits, it is still important that these cities understand the potential for a digital divide that civic tech can produce.
